About
About activity
Total steps

Daily steps are a measure of your daily physical activity. The optimal daily step count depends on your age, body type, fitness level and readiness to perform.

Aim for more than 8,000 steps per day. If you exercise, you can reduce the number of steps accordingly. More steps are not necessarily better — too many can strain knees and ankles, so keeping the count between 8,000 and 15,000 is recommended.

Total calories

Total calories are your total daily energy expenditure — all the calories you burn during the day. Whether you are active or resting, tracking your total calories against your history can help you adjust your intake and maintain a healthy weight.

Although exercise increases your daily calorie consumption, most of the total comes from calories burned at rest — your basal metabolic rate (BMR).

The band starts calculating your total burn in the early morning and records your estimated BMR. The calculation continues until the end of the day, and calories from daily activity are added to the total.

About sleep
How sleep is measured

Your band reads movement, heart rate, heart rate variability and blood oxygen through the night, then infers when you fell asleep, which stage you were in, and when you woke. Readings are estimates, not clinical measurements. This feature is not intended for medical use — speak to a healthcare professional about any concern with your sleep or breathing.

How the score is built

One number from 0 to 100 weighing four things: total time asleep, sleep efficiency (asleep vs. time in bed), how your stages were distributed, and how settled your night was — wake events, restlessness and overnight vitals. Consistency counts too: going to bed and waking at similar times lifts the score even when a single night runs short.

What your score means
86–100Excellent — fully recovered
76–85Good — train and work as planned
60–75Average — keep the day moderate
< 60Poor — prioritise an earlier night
The four stages
Awake

Brief wakings are normal — most nights hold a handful you never remember.

Light

The bulk of the night. Breathing and heart rate settle and the body starts to repair.

REM

Where dreaming happens, and where memory and mood consolidate. Longer stretches come late in the night.

Deep

The hardest to wake from. Tissue repair and immune work concentrate here, mostly in the first half.

Overnight vitals
Heart rate

Should fall through the first half and reach its low point before dawn. A high, flat line often follows a late meal, alcohol or stress.

Heart rate variability

Gaps between beats. Higher usually means better recovery — compare it to your own baseline, never to someone else's.

Skin temperature

Shown as a deviation across the night. A sustained rise can precede illness or follow a hard training day.

Blood oxygen

Typically sits between 95% and 100%. Repeated dips are worth mentioning to a clinician.
